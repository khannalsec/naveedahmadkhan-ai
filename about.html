I am Naveed Ahmad Khan, an applied mathematician and PhD candidate at the University of Canberra, Australia. My supervisor is Associate Professor Shahid Hussain, and my co-supervisor is Professor Girija Chetty. My advisory team includes Professor Wayne Spratford, Professor Roland Goecke, and Professor Prashant K. Jamwal.

My research examines how rehabilitation robots can recognise changes in a person’s effort, stiffness, and contribution to movement and adjust their assistance accordingly. I develop AI and machine learning methods, including self-supervised learning, deep reinforcement learning, transformer networks, and neural operators such as DeepONet and attention-augmented Fourier neural operators. I use these methods to model nonlinear interaction dynamics, generate rehabilitation trajectories, adapt robot impedance, and control velocity and torque.

My doctoral work investigates transactive energy, which describes the energy exchanged between a person and a robot during physical interaction. I develop models to estimate this exchange from measured forces and motion when directly labelled measurements of the underlying energy are unavailable. These estimates help assess active participation and inform control. I combine learned models with biomechanical knowledge and energy-based control and examine their predictive performance, passivity, and stability. I implement and test these methods on ankle, wrist, gait, shoulder, and upper limb rehabilitation systems. This includes developing control software, integrating actuators and sensors, and conducting experiments with healthy participants and people recovering from stroke.

My mathematical research began during my master’s studies under Professor Muhammad Sulaiman at Abdul Wali Khan University, where I worked on neural-network methods for solving differential equations and modelling nonlinear dynamical systems. I subsequently taught mathematics at CECOS University, Pakistan, and worked as a Research Associate under Professor Benzhuo Lu at the Academy of Mathematics and Systems Science, Chinese Academy of Sciences. Alongside my research, I teach and mentor students at the University of Canberra. I completed my PhD final seminar on 22 September 2026.
